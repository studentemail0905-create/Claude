# Playbook — Frameworks, Formulas, Calculators, Scorecards

Deep reference for the Grand Slam Offer Builder. Read the section for the phase you're in. All faithful to Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers*.

---

## Foundational laws (apply throughout)

- **Grow or Die. Maintenance is a myth.** Market grows ~9%/yr; grow ≥9% or fall behind.
- **Commoditized = price-driven** ("they're the same, buy cheaper"). **Differentiated = value-driven** (category of one, no comparison).
- **Money definitions:**
  - **Gross Profit** = revenue − direct cost to service ONE more customer (NOT net profit).
  - **LTV / LTGP** = lifetime *gross* profit per customer (exclude admin/rent/software).
- **Grand Slam Offer = an offer so good people feel stupid saying no.** Built from: attractive promotion + unmatchable value + premium price + unbeatable guarantee + payment terms that fund acquisition.
- **Offer Quality Ladder:** No offer → no business/life · Bad → negative profit, miserable · Decent → no profit, stagnating · Good → okay · **Grand Slam → fantastic profit, freedom.**

## Phase A — Diagnose

- **Two-Problems Triage:** every pain reduces to (1) not enough clients, (2) not enough cash. Root cause = commoditized offer.
- **Commoditization self-test (yes to any = commodity):** Can prospects price-compare you? Are margins thin? Does your marketing look like competitors'? Are you the "second cheapest"?
- **The multiplicative win** of a Grand Slam Offer on the *same* ad spend + fulfillment: higher response × higher conversion × premium price. Worked example: `2.5 × 2.5 × 4 = 22.4×` cash (spent $10k → made $112k).

## Phase B — Market (gate)

### Four Indicators of a Good Market (score great / normal / bad)
1. **Massive Pain** — they desperately *need* it (pain ∝ price; "the pain is the pitch").
2. **Purchasing Power** — they can afford it (don't sell resume help to the unemployed).
3. **Easy to Target** — they congregate in findable places (lists, groups, channels).
4. **Growing** — tailwind, not headwind. **Auto-fail if shrinking** (Lloyd's newspaper software: great product, dying market = pivot).

### Three Evergreen Meta-Markets
**Health · Wealth · Relationships.** Pick a subgroup within one.

### Three Levers (order of importance)
```
Starving Crowd (market)  >  Offer Strength  >  Persuasion Skills
```
A "great" higher lever overpowers a weak lower one; a "bad" lever stops everything unless a higher lever is great.

### Niching — "Riches are in the niches"
Commit to ONE niche; don't niche-hop. Same product, niched down, can charge up to **100×**.

**Niche-Down Pricing Ladder (Dan Kennedy):**
- "Time Management" → $19
- "...for Sales Professionals" → $99
- "...for B2B Outbound Sales Reps" → $499
- "...for B2B Outbound Power-Tools & Gardening Sales Reps" → $1,000–$2,000

**Rule of thumb:** under ~$10M/yr → niche down. Only broaden above ~$10M/yr (based on TAM). Be willing to try up to 100 offers — *"if your offer doesn't work, your offer sucks, not you. You only suck if you stop trying."*

## Phase C — Pricing

### Mindset
- **Never compete on price.** "$0 is the floor; you can go infinitely high the other way." No benefit to being 2nd cheapest; benefit to being most expensive.
- **People buy to get a deal** — when perceived **VALUE > PRICE**. Widen the **price-to-value discrepancy**: raise value first, then price. Sell "money at a discount" ($100k value for $10k).
- **Category of One / monopoly pricing:** price so far above market the buyer assumes "something entirely different is going on here."
- **Profit is oxygen.** "Those who pay the most, pay the most attention" — price so it stings.
- **Gut-check (Dan Kennedy):** "Charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile."

### Calculators (compute with the user's real numbers)

**LTV / LTGP:**
```
LTV = Monthly Revenue × Gross Margin %  × Lifespan (months)
e.g. $1,000 × 90% × 5 = $4,500
```

**Revenue Cap:**
```
Revenue Cap = New Clients per month × LTV
e.g. 10 × $1,000 = $10,000/mo max
```

### Virtuous Cycle of Price (raising)
emotional investment ↑ → perceived value ↑ → results ↑ → best clients attracted → margin ↑ → reinvest → grow.

### Vicious Cycle (lowering) — the reverse
"didn't cost much" → low perceived value → poor results → worst clients (never satisfied) → margin destroyed → race to the bottom.

### Anti-pattern: "Broke Competitor" pricing (flag if detected)
look at market → average everyone → price slightly below → "more for less." Don't.

### Conviction
Document client results so you can price without flinching. Reframe close: "If I made you $239,000 this year, would you pay me $42,000?"

## Phase D — The Value Equation

```
          Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value  =  ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
          Perceived Time Delay  ×  Perceived Effort & Sacrifice
```

**Four drivers:**
- **Dream Outcome** — *increase* (gap between their reality and dreams; desire is channeled, not created).
- **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement** — *increase* (people pay for certainty).
- **Perceived Time Delay** — *decrease* (long-term outcome + short-term experience).
- **Perceived Effort & Sacrifice** — *decrease* (tangible + intangible costs).

It's **division** → drive the bottom toward zero and value approaches infinity. Beginners chase bigger claims (the lazy top); **the best compete on the bottom** (immediate, seamless, effortless — and defensible).

**Perception is reality** — communicate each driver; prefer **psychological** over **logical** solutions (logical ones are already tried).

**Status:** the dream outcome that most raises their standing *vs others* is valued most.

### Four Prospect Questions (map to drivers)
- What will I make? → Dream Outcome
- How will I know it'll happen? → Likelihood
- How long will it take? → Time
- What's expected of me? → Effort & Sacrifice

### Binary Value Scoring
Score the offer 1/0 on each of the four drivers; sum. Use to compare two vehicles with the *same* dream outcome (Meditation vs Xanax; Bootcamp vs Liposuction).

### Speed beats free
"The only thing that beats 'free' is 'fast.'" Sell **done-for-you** over DIY. Engineer a **fast emotional win** within the first days.

## Phase E — Build the Offer

### Part I — Problems & Solutions
**Step 1 — Dream Outcome:** "[big result] in [short time]" (e.g. "Lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks"). Sell the vacation, not the plane flight.

**Step 2 — List Problems (sequence walk):**
1. List each thing the customer must DO, in sequence (Buy food → Cook → Eat → Exercise).
2. For each action, list every reason they can't / won't, using the four drivers as prompts:
   - not worth it → Dream Outcome
   - won't work for me / can't stick to it → Likelihood
   - too hard / confusing → Effort & Sacrifice
   - takes too long / too busy → Time
3. **Target ~16 core × 2–4 sub = 32–64 problems.** "The more problems you find, the more you get to solve."

**Step 3 — Problem → Solution:** put **"How to"** in front + **reverse** the problem into the solved outcome.
- "...takes too much time" → "How to buy healthy food *quickly*"
- "...is expensive" → "How to buy healthy food *for less than your current grocery bill*"
- **Coverage rule:** solutions must hit **all four drivers**. A missing one can block the sale.

### Part II — Trim & Stack
**Mantra: "Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction."**

**Step 4 — Delivery vehicles (6 cheat codes):**
1. Personal attention: 1-on-1 / small group / one-to-many
2. Effort expected: DIY / DWY (done-with-you) / DFY (done-for-you)
3. If live: in-person / phone / email / text / Zoom / chat
4. If recorded: audio / video / written
5. Speed & availability: which days, hours, response time
6. **10× / 1-10th test:** If they paid 10× ($100k), what would you add? If they paid 1/10th and you had to make it *more* valuable, how?

**Step 5a — Trim (2×2: cost-to-you × value-to-customer):**
1. Remove **high-cost, low-value** first.
2. Then **low-cost, low-value**.
3. Keep low-cost/high-value AND high-cost/high-value.
4. Prioritize **one-to-many, high-value** assets (high one-time cost, ~zero marginal cost, value forever — "why software is so valuable").

**Step 5b — Stack:**
```
Problem → Solution wording → Sexier bundle name
  (list the actual delivery vehicles underneath)
  (assign a $ value to each bundle, anchored to money/time saved)
  → sum to Total Value, then reveal the (much lower) Price
```
**Bundle quality check:** solves ALL problems (before/during/after) · feels one-of-a-kind · uncomparable to competitors → forces a value-based decision.

## Phase F — Enhance

### Intro: shift the demand curve
All marketing = artificially **increase demand** + **decrease perceived supply**. Perfect Profit Combination = lots of demand + little perceived supply. "Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want" (Naval). **Hormozi's Law: "The longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make."** Always leave pent-up demand; skim the top (one-fifth will pay 5×).

### F1 — Scarcity (function of quantity)
"When demand increases, cut supply." "Fear of loss > desire for gain." Scarcity implies social proof ("81% to capacity").

**Service scarcity methods:** Total Business Cap (only X clients ever) · Growth-Rate Cap (X/week) · Cohort Cap (X per class).
**Physical:** Limited Release — announce a fixed quantity, always sell out, repeat (~monthly), tell everyone you sold out.
**Honest scarcity (most ethical):** pick a real number → advertise it → show progress to capacity → let FOMO work. *Only you draw where "full" is.*

### F2 — Urgency (function of time)
"Deadlines. Drive. Decisions." Must be **real**. 50–60% of launch sales come in the **last 4 hours** of the last day.

**Four ethical methods:**
1. **Cohort rolling** — start in groups on a cadence; sign up now or wait.
2. **Seasonal rolling** — same promo, new seasonal name with real start/end dates.
3. **Pricing/bonus-based** — the *promo/discount/bonus* is what expires (lets year-round sellers use urgency honestly).
4. **Exploding opportunity** — time-decaying arbitrage (new ad platform, etc.).

### F3 — Bonuses
"A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into parts and stacked as bonuses." Anchor price to the **core**; pile bonuses until the discrepancy is too big to bear. **Never discount the core** (teaches negotiability).

**Bonus Bullets (11 rules):**
1. Always offer them. 2. Name each with a benefit in the title. 3. Tell them how it relates / what it is / how you made it / how it improves life. 4. Give proof. 5. Paint a vivid after-image. 6. Ascribe a price tag. 7. Prefer tools & checklists over more trainings (lower effort/time). 8. Each addresses a specific objection. 9. Solve their *next* problem in advance. 10. Bonus value should **eclipse the core**. 11. Add scarcity/urgency to the bonuses themselves.

**1-on-1 sequencing:** ask for the sale FIRST → if yes, reveal bonuses after (wow) → if no, present a bonus matching their objection, ask again ("fair enough?").

**OPP (other people's products):** get non-competing businesses to give bonuses free for exposure; negotiate a group discount + referral commission to you.

### F4 — Guarantees (reverse the risk)
"The single greatest objection is risk." Better guarantee → 2–4× conversion.

**Teeth formula:**
```
"If you don't get [X result] in [Y time], we will [Z remedy]."
```
The "or what" (Z) is what gives it teeth.

**Four types:**
1. **Unconditional** — refund if not satisfied (strongest pull, most refunds; best low-ticket B2C).
2. **Conditional** — remedy only if they did the work; aim for "better than money back." *Ideal: they qualify but succeeded so don't claim.*
3. **Anti-guarantee** — "all sales final," with a creative "reason why" (for consumables / things that lose value once seen).
4. **Implied** — performance-based (revshare, profit-share, triggers). No performance, no pay.

**Guarantee ROI math (decide by math, not emotion):**
```
Baseline:       100 sales, 5 refunds (5%)   = 95 net
With guarantee: 130 sales, 13 refunds (10%) = 117 net
117 / 95 = 1.23×  → 23% more money, all to the bottom line
```
Rule: a guarantee only fails to pay off if the extra sales are 100% offset by an equal *absolute* rise in refunds (usually a doubling — unlikely).

**Create one:** name their biggest fears → ask "what do they NOT want to happen if they pay?" → reverse those fears into the guarantee → make it specific/creative → name it ("Club a Baby Seal Guarantee"). Caveat: guarantees amplify a good offer, can't save a bad one; tie conditions to required client work to filter freeloaders.

### F5 — Naming (MAGIC)
"We're not changing the offer, just the wrapping paper." Offers fatigue over years (local markets faster). Implicit-egotism: call out the avatar.

**MAGIC (use 3–5 of 5, whatever's punchiest):**
- **M — Magnetic reason-why** (Free, 88% Off, Giveaway, Grand Opening, New Year…)
- **A — Announce the avatar** (hyper-local: "Bee Cave Dentists," "Rolling Hills Moms")
- **G — Give a goal** (the dream outcome: "Double Your Profit," "Pain Free")
- **I — Indicate a time interval** ("4 Hour," "21 Day," "6 Week")
- **C — Complete with a container word** (Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Intensive, Incubator, Masterclass, Accelerator, Sprint, Transformation, Mastermind, Reset)

**⚠ Compliance:** never pair a quantifiable claim (income/weight) with a duration → implies a guarantee. "$10,000 in 10 days" (risky) → "Make Your First Sale in 10 Days" (safe).

**Offer Fatigue — change the wrapper before the machine (lightest → heaviest):**
1 creative → 2 body copy → 3 headline → 4 name goal/theme → 5 seasonality/angle → 6 duration → 7 enhancer → 8 monetization structure (last resort).

**Milestone:** first $100,000 in *personal* accounts. Runway = savings ÷ annual living expenses.

**11-Point "In a Nutshell" completeness check:**
1. Don't be a commodity. 2. Pick a normal/growing market — niche down. 3. Charge a lot. 4. Charge using the 4 value drivers. 5. Create the value offer in 5 steps. 6. Stack value, deliver, make it profitable. 7. Add scarcity. 8. Add urgency. 9. Add bonuses. 10. Reverse risk with a guarantee. 11. Name it for your avatar.
